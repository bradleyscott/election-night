The complete count · four ways to say it

proposal

The card the page wears from election night until the official count: the count has stopped, so the number has to stop too. Every card below holds Banks Peninsula at the same moment — Weenink ahead by 310, 80% of the expected final vote in, about 9,700 special votes still to be counted — at the 512px the detail column gives it on a 1400px page.

Today the card says Tonight’s count complete and stops there, which is the one thing it should not do quietly: a complete night count is not a result, and Banks Peninsula in 2023 was decided by 396 votes after the specials were added. The tokens, utilities and component classes are the real ones; the markup is hand-drawn.

ProvenanceThe seat, the final margin (396 votes) and the share of the vote that arrives as specials (21.0% in 2023) are real. The archive holds only the final files, so the night’s own candidate votes were reconstructed from them by removing the specials in proportion — the preliminary numbers are a construction, and the margin they produce is the right order of magnitude rather than a record. Every card carries the same figures, so only the design moves.

ConstraintThe percentage that leads the counting card is a probability; here it would have to read 100%, so the card has to lead with something else — the winner, the margin, or the count itself. And whatever it leads with, it owes the reader the one fact that can still change the seat: the specials.

Party viewUnchanged: the panel renders nothing in party mode, in either state. A party can contest the party vote in a seat it nominates no candidate for, so neither a probability nor a winner belongs there.

Changedthe counting card and this one are the same card at two moments, so the eye does not have to relearn the panel when the count stops. The 4xl number changes meaning — a chance while counting, a share of the votes once counted — but the label above it changes too, and the bars keep their job: showing who is ahead of whom.

Costthe strip’s bar means something else here — 80% of the vote the seat will eventually cast, not of the vote expected tonight — so the label moves under the bar and the two ends carry plain counts instead of a percentage each. The rows also lose the even tick the counting card draws: half the votes is not the threshold a completed count is judged against, and the other seven candidates take 28% of the vote the bars are a share of.

What the 2023 result saysBanks Peninsula finished 17,860 to 17,464 — a margin of 396, 0.8% of 48,891 votes, with 83.8% of enrolled voters voting. Before the specials those same votes sat 310 apart. Two weeks moved the seat, not its party, but a card that hid the specials would have been silent through the only fortnight in which this seat was still undecided.

If A is chosenthe strip needs one more field on the complete chance — specialsOutstanding, which computeSeatChance already computes and currently discards when it returns state: 'complete'. Nothing else on the card needs a new number: the votes, the share and the margin are all in the payload.

Recommendation

A, because the panel needs no second vocabulary when the count stops: the rows, the bars and the margin line are already the ones the reader has been looking at all night, and the only new fact — the specials — arrives in the slot the outstanding votes just left. It also keeps the runner-up on the card, which is the comparison a 310-vote seat turns on. B’s closing block is worth stealing for A’s caption: not final is the sentence the card has to leave behind.
