Electorates · the map's own controls

proposal

The three controls that act on the map — seat search, the General/Māori switch and Clear — move out of the page header and into the map panel. Nothing else moves: the map keeps its height, the legend and attribution keep the footer strip, and the detail column is untouched. Drawn at 1400px page width, so the two-column map is 656px — the width the running app gives it at about 1440px. The two-column panels show the state after Banks Peninsula is clicked, framed the way fitBounds frames it, because that is the state the header's controls are for; C is the landing state, the one panel that holds the whole country. The geometry and the fills are the real 2023 general seats, shaded by chance of winning as the map does today.

One hairline card of the map's own vocabulary, so the header reverts to just the title. The zoom control moves to the top-right because the corner it normally takes is the controls'. The card is stacked at z-[500] — above the map's panes (400), below the corners Leaflet stacks its controls in (1000) — so the search list paints over the geometry instead of under it. Below 1000 it would slide under the zoom buttons instead.

The cost is real: after a click the seat's bounds fill the frame, so the card sits over whatever the fit put in that corner — here the Christchurch seats beside Banks Peninsula, and for a seat whose bounds hug the top-left (a tall northern seat, a wide coastal one) the seat itself. Nothing breaks, but the card covers geometry the click just asked to see, and the search list covers more of it while it is open.

The controls join the panel instead of the page: a hairline row above the map, the map's whole height and every corner still free. No geometry is ever covered, the seat that fitBounds centres is never behind a card, and the phone layout is the same markup at 390px rather than a second design — search on its own row, the switch and Clear below it. Clear returns to the right edge, where it was.

The row costs the card 56px — the controls are 44px hit targets, plus the row's own padding — and the controls lose the title's line. But at sm and below they already wrapped to their own row, so the phone gains nothing and loses nothing. The row carries z-[1100]: the open search list has to clear the corners Leaflet stacks its zoom buttons in (1000), and the map's panes are at 400, so anything lower puts the list under one or the other.

A 300px map is the argument against the overlay: the card takes a sixth of it, and on a fitted seat that sixth is the seat.
