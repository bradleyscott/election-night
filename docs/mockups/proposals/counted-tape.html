Proposal · Counted charts Not implemented — design capture

Counted, drawn as a tape.

Counted is the only chart in the app that is not print: a green monotone line over a dashed grid, drawn identically on a seat and on the country. This evolves both of them into the vocabulary of the night tape from the voting-places proposal: the same green, drawn as a step, with receipt ticks, mono numerals and hairline rules. It gives each surface the element only it can have.

A seat has voting-place receipts, so its tape carries a rug of them. The country has only snapshots, so its tape steps between scrapes. Both stop at the last one — a count is a record, not a forecast.

Same series, same green

#22c55e is what Counted wears today, in the line and in the legend. The tape changes the drawing, not the colour, so the chart and the mode button beside it stay one thing.

No cadence

A monotone curve hides how the night actually arrives: advance votes at seven, the cliff after ten, then a tail of specials that is measured in weeks.

Reads as a forecast

A smoothed line suggests the next value follows from the last. The count arrives in chunks, so the chart should look like a record of what has landed, not a trend to be extended.

Seat looks like country

One seat has per-place receipts; the country has only totals. Drawing both the same way throws away the difference between them.

Shows

The step is the seat's counted votes. The ticks underneath are the seat's own voting places, each drawn at the moment its count landed — so a flat stretch of step has a visible cause ("three halls at once", "nothing since 9pm") instead of looking like a stalled line.

Needs

Nothing new. Receipt times come from useVotingPlaces (shipped with the map layer); the step comes from the electorate history the chart already receives. Nothing is drawn past the last receipt, so the tape never claims to know a vote that has not landed.

Breaks when

A seat with three places is a rug of three ticks and carries no news; the step has to stand alone. And a place's receipt time says nothing about how many votes it holds, so a big hall and a church hall pull the same weight on the rug. The tape also stops where the count stops, which on a slow seat can be half past nine.

Shows

The half of election night a map is bad at: how fast it is going. The step is the national share counted, drawn at the minute each scrape landed, so the advance vote, the nine o'clock lull and the ten o'clock cliff keep their shape instead of being sanded into a curve.

Breaks when

A missed scrape reads as a longer flat step, not a gap — there is no interpolation to hide it. And the right edge is always slightly behind the room: it is the last scrape, not the last ballot, and nothing marks it, because there is nowhere further to go.

The vocabulary, borrowed whole
Counted

Solid step in the series green. No smoothing, because the count jumps between polls and a curve would invent the in-between.

Place reported

One tick per voting place at its receipt time, on the baseline. Seat only — it is the rug we can finally afford.

If it ships

Two branches, no new dependency.

VoteHistoryChart.tsx — the chartMode === 'counted' branch swaps its Line for the seat tape; history and receipts are already props-adjacent, so nothing new is fetched.

Trends.tsx — the counted branch draws the national tape from chartData, stepping between consecutive points and stopping at the last one. No new data is needed on either page.

Hand-drawn SVG, as here: no chart library, no animation, no tooltip library. Values are printed in mono next to the mark, so hovering is a nicety rather than the only way to read a number — which also makes the whole chart legible to a screen reader from its aria-label summary.

Dark mode is free: every mark is a token (foreground, border, primary), not a literal.

Provenance. A synthetic night, not any real cycle: advance votes land just after seven and the count climbs through the evening. Seat receipts are the kind of timestamps the live per-place files carry; the seat itself (Epsom) is only a label. Marks, type, rules and spacing are the shipped design tokens — IBM Plex Mono for numbers, Inter for labels, Playfair for the heading — so what you see is what a component would print.
